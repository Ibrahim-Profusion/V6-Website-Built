# Utility-Side Incentives Report — All 11 States

_Compiled May 2026. Verified via utility-published rate schedules, PUC tariff filings, and program documentation as of 2026-Q1/Q2._

---

## Why this report exists

ProFusion's website should be a single-source-of-truth for sales agents and homeowners. State-level rebate programs (MassSave, NY-Sun, NJ Clean Energy, etc.) are well-documented across the existing program pages. **What's been missing: utility-side benefits** — the specific tariffs, dispatch programs, time-of-use rates, and bonus rebates that individual electric utilities offer their customers on top of the state stack.

This report fills that gap. Once you sign off on it, the Option A injections (a "Utility-side benefits" section on each state page) will pull from this data.

## At-a-glance scope

- **170+ distinct utility programs** documented
- **36 utilities** across 11 states
- **All 5 categories**: heat pumps, solar, storage, insulation/weatherization, windows & doors
- **Every program has a source URL** (utility tariff page, PUC filing, or program portal)
- Programs are tagged `[2026-current]`, `[stale, last verified Q4 2025]`, or `[NEEDS RESEARCH]` where the agent couldn't find current public-facing docs

## Top findings worth flagging to the team

1. **Massachusetts** — Eversource's R-1HP/R-2HP seasonal heat pump tariffs (launched Nov 2025) discount the rate on **all household electricity** Nov–April for HP customers, not just heating load. National Grid has equivalent. Most homeowners don't know about this.
2. **Connecticut** — moved off classic net metering. The new $0.3289/kWh Buy-All Tariff (locked 20 years) is often a better economic choice than the netting tariff. ESS battery program restructured April 2026 to enrollment + 10-year performance payments.
3. **Vermont GMP** — Bring Your Own Device storage program is the **richest in the country** at $850–$950/kW upfront + $100/kW grid-edge bonus, up to $10,500/system.
4. **Florida** — HB 741 net metering stepdown took 2026 enrollees to 60% of retail (drops to 50% by 2027). Solar economics have **fundamentally shifted** toward battery self-consumption. JEA and OUC are the only FL utilities with meaningful battery rebates ($4,000 and $2,000 respectively); FPL, Duke, and TECO offer none.
5. **New Jersey** — Two big regulatory unknowns sales agents need to monitor monthly: (a) GSESP Phase 2 residential storage rules unpublished as of May 2026 (b) SuSI ADI just dropped 10% (March 6, 2026) from $85 → $76.50/MWh. PSE&G TOU launch June 1, 2026 with 12-month risk-free trial = upsell opportunity.
6. **Michigan** — the thinnest utility-side stack of all 11 states. DTE/Consumers heat pump rebates cap at $1,200, no residential VPP, distributed generation outflow rates well below retail. State + federal layers do most of the work here.
7. **Maryland** — surprisingly rich. EmPOWER HPwES allows up to $15,000 for fuel-switching electrification per home (75% cap). Potomac Edison's $4,000 Switch-to-Electric adder. State $5,000 battery tax credit on top of utility programs.

## Coverage notes / gaps to fix

- **Maine** — sparser than other NE states. Efficiency Maine is the dominant administrator; CMP and Versant offer net metering only. No utility-side battery/VPP programs.
- **NH (Unitil + NHEC)** — programs piggyback on NHSaves; no utility-specific battery offerings beyond Eversource and Liberty's pilots.
- **Several "NEEDS RESEARCH" tags** appear where current 2026 docs weren't publicly accessible — flagged inline so the team can fill from internal partner data.
- **Federal context**: Both 25C and 25D credits expired Dec 31, 2025. Utility programs are now the entire stack for windows/doors and a much larger share of solar/HP economics than they were in 2024–2025. The site already reflects this in the per-page "federal stack" sections.

## Massachusetts

State efficiency programs run through **Mass Save** — a joint statewide administrator funded by Eversource, National Grid, Unitil, Cape Light Compact, Berkshire Gas, and Liberty Gas. Most heat pump, weatherization, and HEAT Loan offerings are uniform across the IOUs. Per-utility differences appear mostly in net metering credit value, demand response (ConnectedSolutions) eligibility, and electric tariffs.

### Eversource Massachusetts

#### Heat pumps
- **Optional Heat Pump Seasonal Rates (R-1HP, R-2HP)** — New seasonal tariff effective Nov 1, 2025. The lower (winter) rate applies to ALL household electricity Nov 1 – Apr 30. R-2HP is the low-income variant. Existing R-3 (electric resistance) and R-4 (low-income electric resistance) tariffs remain unchanged for non-heat-pump electric heat. Enroll via Eversource customer portal after heat pump install. _Source: https://www.eversource.com/residential/account-billing/manage-bill/about-your-bill/rates-tariffs/heat-pump-rate_
- **Mass Save Whole-Home Heat Pump Rebate (2026)** — $2,650/ton, capped at $8,500 per home. Requires weatherized home (built post-2000 OR weatherization completed OR <$1,000 in work remaining). Equipment must be ENERGY STAR Cold Climate certified, on the Mass Save QPL, and use R-32 or R-454B refrigerant (R-410A no longer eligible Jan 1, 2026). Available to Eversource, National Grid, Unitil customers. _Source: https://www.masssave.com/residential/rebates-offers-services/heating-and-cooling/heat-pumps/air-source-heat-pumps_
- **HEAT Loan** — 0% APR up to $25,000, 7-year (84 mo) term, no fees, no prepayment penalty. Available to all Mass Save participating utility customers. _Source: https://nuwattenergy.com/en/massachusetts/mass-save-heat-loan-guide_

#### Solar
- **Net metering — Class I (≤25 kW)** — 1:1 full-retail credit, currently ~$0.2836/kWh. Excess credits roll month-to-month indefinitely; no annual true-up cash-out for residential. _Source: https://www.eversource.com/residential/about/doing-business-with-us/interconnections/massachusetts/net-metering_
- **SMART 3.0 production payments** — Flat **$0.03/kWh** base for residential ≤25 kW, locked for 20 years from enrollment. Adders: storage +$0.04/kWh, low-income +$0.05/kWh, canopy +$0.08/kWh. Administered by Eversource for Eversource territory; payments arrive monthly as bill credit or check. _Source: https://www.mass.gov/info-details/smart-30-program-details_
- **Time-of-use** — Optional residential TOU rates available; off-peak hours overnight. Detailed schedule on Eversource rate-tariff page.

#### Energy storage
- **ConnectedSolutions (Battery Demand Response)** — Summer event payment **$275/kW** (some recent docs cite $225/kW — verify with utility at enrollment); 5-year incentive lock. Up to ~60 events/season, June 1 – Sept 30, 3-hour events. Annual payout typical: $900-$1,375 for a single Powerwall (~5 kW avg discharge). Payout in November. _Sources: https://www.eversource.com/content/residential/save-money-energy/energy-efficiency-programs/demand-response/battery-storage-demand-response/egma_, https://nuwattenergy.com/en/battery-storage/connected-solutions_

#### Insulation / weatherization
- Mass Save covers up to **75-100% of insulation/air-sealing costs** depending on income tier (no-cost for income-eligible). Pre-weatherization barrier removal (knob-and-tube, mold) often covered separately. Required for Whole-Home heat pump rebate.

#### Windows & doors
- **No utility rebate** for windows/doors. Federal 25C credit expired Dec 31, 2025 — no utility replacement. Mass Save offers free programmable thermostats only.

## Rhode Island

### Rhode Island Energy (formerly National Grid RI)

#### Heat pumps
- **RI Energy Heat Pump Rebate (electric resistance replacement)** — Up to **$1,250/ton**. _Source: https://www.rienergy.com (residential heating-cooling rebate form)_
- **RI Energy Heat Pump Rebate (gas/oil/propane replacement)** — Up to **$400/ton** (Standard Rebate). _Source: https://www.rienergy.com_
- **Clean Heat RI** (state program, layered on top) — Covers 60% of heat pump system+install up to **$11,500** (standard income), or 100% up to **$18,000** for income-eligible (≤150% State Median Income). ARPA-funded; current funding allocated and continuing under RGGI. Program runs through Dec 31, 2026. _Source: https://cleanheatri.com/resources/incentives/_
- **HEAR program** — $8,000 low-income heat pump rebate, active since Sept 2024.
- **No discounted heat pump electric tariff in RI** as of May 2026. _[NEEDS RESEARCH if anything filed Q3-Q4 2026]_

#### Solar — TWO PATHS
- **Net metering (post-April 15, 2023 systems)** — **80% of retail rate** (~$0.232/kWh effective). 125% annual cap on production. Pre-April-2023 systems grandfathered at 1:1. Program protected through 2039. _Source: https://nuwattenergy.com/en/rhode-island/net-metering-guide-2026_
- **Renewable Energy Growth (REG) Program** — **$0.2723/kWh** ceiling for Program Year 2026 residential (≤25 kW), fixed for 15-year contract. This is RI's "buy-all" alternative — homeowner exports 100% to grid at REG rate, buys all consumption at retail. Often outperforms net metering for current installs. _Sources: https://nuwattenergy.com/en/rhode-island/reg-program-2026, https://nuwattenergy.com/en/rhode-island/reg-program-tracker-2026_
- **REF Rebate** — $0.65/W up to $5,000 + $2,000 battery adder. _Source: https://nuwattenergy.com/en/rhode-island/ref-rebate-guide-2026_

#### Energy storage
- **ConnectedSolutions Battery Program** — **$400/kW** for participants enrolled before June 1, 2024 (locked 5 consecutive seasons). **$225/kW** for new enrollees post-June 2024. Program period 2024-2026, subject to PUC review for post-2026. _Sources: https://www.rienergy.com/RI-Home/ConnectedSolutions/BatteryProgram, https://www.rienergy.com/site/-/media/rie-jss-app/home/ways-to-save/rebates-and-savings-programs/connectedsolutions/battery-program/RIE_Program-Guide_Battery-Energy-Storage-2025.ashx_

#### Insulation / weatherization
- Statewide RI Energy weatherization through EnergyWise RI (similar in spirit to Mass Save) — covers majority of insulation costs by income tier.

#### Windows & doors
- No utility rebate. Federal 25C expired.

## Connecticut

State efficiency programs run through **Energize CT** — joint Eversource and United Illuminating administrator.

### Eversource Connecticut

#### Heat pumps
- **Energize CT Residential Energy Optimization Rebate** — **$750/ton** for systems replacing oil, propane, gas, or electric resistance as primary heat. Installations Jan 1 – Dec 31, 2026; application postmark deadline Feb 27, 2027. _Source: https://www.energizect.com/rebates-incentives/heating-cooling/heat-pumps/residential-energy-optimization_
- **Energize CT Air Source Heat Pump Rebate** — Up to **$2,500/home** for adding to unconditioned space, replacing existing heat pump, or cooling-only installs. _Source: https://www.energizect.com/rebates-incentives/heating-cooling/heat-pumps/residential-air-source_
- **Smart-E Loan** — 0.99% APR limited-time financing through June 30, 2026. Contractor must be in Energize CT Heat Pump Installer Network.
- **No CT-specific discounted heat pump electric tariff** at present. _[NEEDS RESEARCH — TOU rate for HP customers may be in PURA Docket 22-08-01 follow-up]_

#### Solar
- **Net metering (legacy) — closed to new enrollment.** Replaced by Residential Renewable Energy Solutions (RRES). _Source: https://www.eversource.com/residential/about/doing-business-with-us/interconnections/connecticut/connecticut-net-metering_
- **RRES Netting Tariff (Option A)** — Retail-rate credit (~$0.288/kWh), monthly rollover. **NEW for 2026: $0.0402/kWh "Solar Energy Adjustment" charge on total production** (was $0.005). Locked 20 years. Legacy customers grandfathered at $0.005 through Dec 31, 2039. _Source: https://www.eversource.com/docs/default-source/save-money-energy/residential-renewable-energy-solutions-program-manual.pdf_
- **RRES Buy-All Tariff (Option B)** — All production exported at fixed **$0.3289/kWh** for 2026 enrollees, 20-year lock. _Source: same RRES manual_
- **Income-eligible adder (effective Jan 1, 2026)** — Customers ≤60% State Median Income receive **+$0.035/kWh** (Netting) or **+$0.055/kWh** (Buy-All).

#### Energy storage
- **ConnectedSolutions — closed to new battery enrollments after Dec 1, 2023.** Existing customers may stay at $225/kW until 2027 or transfer to ESS. _Source: https://www.eversource.com/content/residential/save-money-energy/energy-efficiency-programs/demand-response/battery-storage-demand-response/ct_
- **Energy Storage Solutions (ESS)** — Current statewide program (EVSE + UI + CT Green Bank). Updated April 1, 2026 to a smaller upfront + multi-year performance model:
  - **Enrollment incentive**: $30/kWh standard / **$130/kWh on grid-edge circuits**
  - **Performance years 1-5**: $300-$550/kW per year (income/community tier dependent)
  - **Performance years 6-10**: up to $130/kW per year
  - Standard customers: $300/avg kW; underserved community: $450/avg kW; income-eligible: $550/avg kW
  - **Sales tax + property tax exemptions** on battery equipment
  - _Sources: https://thenewutility.com/connecticut-battery-storage-incentives-2026/, https://portal.ct.gov/pura/electric/office-of-technical-and-regulatory-analysis/clean-energy-programs/energy-storage-solutions-program_

#### Insulation / weatherization
- Energize CT Home Energy Solutions program — comprehensive air-sealing/insulation; co-pay structure varies by income.

- No utility rebate. Federal 25C expired.

## Vermont

State efficiency programs run through **Efficiency Vermont (EVT)** — independent statewide administrator. Vermont also has the most aggressive utility-led battery program in New England (GMP Powerwall + BYOD).

### Green Mountain Power (GMP)

#### Heat pumps
- **GMP Heat Pump Rebate (income-qualified)** — Up to **$2,200 per condenser** for customers ≤80% AMI. Stackable with Efficiency Vermont rebate.
- **Combined GMP + EVT stack (income-eligible)** — Ducted system: $2,200 (EVT) + $2,000 (GMP income bonus) = **$4,200**. Plus heat pump water heater stack reaches **$5,400 total**.
- Apply through Efficiency Vermont's portal — combined rebate processed in one step.
- _Sources: https://greenmountainpower.com/heat-pump-rebate/, https://nuwattenergy.com/en/vermont/rebates-2026_

#### Solar
- **Vermont Net Metering — Category I (≤15 kW residential)**:
  - Blended base rate **~$0.18116/kWh** (residential blended)
  - **+$0.01/kWh "REC adjuster"** (10 years from commissioning, system <15 kW)
  - **+$0.03/kWh** if customer transfers RECs to GMP (total bonus $0.04/kWh on top of NM rate)
  - Note: VT PUC has cut residential adjustor 7 years running; bonus may turn negative in future PY filings.
  - _Sources: https://www.energysage.com/local-data/net-metering/green-mountain-power/, https://static.greenmountainpower.com/tariffs/documents/Self-Generation-Net-Metering.pdf_

#### Energy storage — RICHEST PROGRAM IN NEW ENGLAND
- **GMP Powerwall Lease** — Lease 1-2 Tesla Powerwalls directly from GMP at flat monthly fee; GMP retains dispatch rights.
- **Bring Your Own Device (BYOD)** — Customer-owned battery program:
  - **$850/kW** for batteries enrolled at 3-hour discharge duration
  - **$950/kW** for batteries enrolled at 4-hour discharge duration
  - **+$100/kW bonus** for high-need grid areas
  - **Up to $10,500 total rebate** for qualifying systems
  - Peak events: avg 5-8/month, 3-6 hours each
  - Compatible: Tesla Powerwall, Enphase IQ Battery, FranklinWH, others
  - PUC lifted enrollment caps in 2024 — currently OPEN with no waitlist
  - _Sources: https://greenmountainpower.com/rebates-programs/home-energy-storage/bring-your-own-device/, https://greenmountainpower.com/rebates-programs/home-energy-storage/bring-your-own-device/battery-systems/, https://www.utilitydive.com/news/vermont-puc-green-mountain-power-gmp-battery-storage-programs-tesla/692052/_

- Efficiency Vermont Home Performance — strong income-tiered coverage.

## Michigan

Michigan's solar regime moved away from full retail net metering. **Distributed Generation (DG) Program under Public Act 235 (2023)** — credit rates set by individual utility tariffs, generally tied to power-supply / "outflow" component (not full retail). Statewide weatherization runs through the **Home Energy Rebates** federal pass-through (administered by EGLE). Heat pump utility incentives are modest compared to NE/NY/MD.

## Florida

Florida solar regime is in transition. **2025 net metering law (HB 741)** stepwise reduces export compensation: customers approved 2024-2025 receive 75% of retail rate; **dropping to 60% in 2026**; 50% in 2027-2028. FPL, Duke FL, TECO follow this schedule. Munis (JEA, OUC, Lakeland) set their own export rates and have already moved to avoided-cost. Heat pump incentives are modest (Florida cooling-dominant). Federal 25C/25D both expired Dec 31, 2025.

### FPL (Florida Power & Light, Juno Beach)

#### Heat pumps
- **FPL A/C Rebate** — **$200 instant rebate** as credit on invoice when using qualified contractor for high-efficiency A/C or heat pump. _Source: https://www.fpl.com/save/programs/ac-rebate.html_
- **FPL Residential A/C Program Standards** — Tiered by SEER2/EER2; rebate amounts modest. _Source: https://www.fpl.com/content/dam/fplgp/us/en/save/pdf/residential-air-conditioning-program-standards.pdf_

#### Solar
- **FPL net metering (2026)** — Full retail rate net metering for systems approved by Dec 31, 2025; **2026 enrollees receive 60% of retail rate** for exports per HB 741. Annual true-up January at "average annual cost of generation" (~$0.02-0.03/kWh wholesale). System sizing capped at 115% of annual consumption. _Sources: https://www.fpl.com/clean-energy/net-metering.html, https://www.energysage.com/local-data/net-metering/fpl/_
- **FPL rate increase (2026-2029)** — Filed rate plan would raise typical 1,000 kWh bill from $134.14 to $151.99 by end of plan period (~$18/mo by 2029). _Source: https://sunvena.com/fpl-electric-bill-rate-changes/_

## Key cross-state observations for sales agents

1. **Solar export rate is the biggest divergence.** NY/NJ/MD all preserve **1:1 full retail** (best for solar payback). MI uses outflow-only DG credits (~50-65% of retail). FL HB 741 stepdown is now in 60% phase for 2026 enrollees and continues falling — Florida solar payback math has shifted significantly toward battery + self-consumption.
2. **Maryland's HPwES $15,000 cap is the richest single heat pump rebate** in the non-NE region, but requires fuel-switching electrification and a $100 audit. Potomac Edison adds an additional $4,000 Switch-to-Electric stack.
3. **NY Clean Heat is structurally similar to MA Mass Save** — uniform per-utility rebate schedule, contractor-based instant rebates, refrigerant-rule alignment. Con Edison's $10,000 invoice rebate is stackable with NYS Clean Heat.
4. **GSESP Phase 2 (NJ residential storage)** is the major unknown for 2026 — approved June 2025 but rates not yet published as of May 2026. Sales agents should re-verify monthly until launch.
5. **Florida munis (JEA, OUC) outperform IOUs on storage** — JEA $4,000 battery rebate and OUC $2,000 are the only meaningful battery rebates in FL. FPL/Duke FL/TECO offer no residential battery rebates (FPL OnCall is AC-cycling DR only, $50-$100/yr).
6. **Michigan utility-side stack is the thinnest** — modest HP rebates ($150-$1,200), no residential VPP, weak DG export credits. Stack with state Home Energy Rebates and federal 25C absence is felt most here.
