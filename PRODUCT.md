## North Star

**The Trusted Handyman, Engineering-Backed.**

Picture the wise, weathered guy in the neighborhood that everyone goes to before they say yes to a heat pump, or a roof, or a furnace. He's seen 1,200 homes. He's done the math. He explains things plainly enough that a five-year-old gets it. He doesn't pitch, he doesn't push, and he doesn't talk down.

Under the surface, we are engineers. We compute scores against a real underlying model, we keep utility tariffs and rebate programs synced weekly, and we audit installer quality on six gates per job. On the surface, we're the trusted neighbor who looks at your house and tells you, in plain English, what to do first and why.

That tension is the whole brand. **Engineers under the hood. Handyman at the door.**

## Users

The primary visitor is a stressed homeowner with a high utility bill in hand, on a phone, in the evening. They arrived from a Google search after opening that bill. They are skeptical of sales pitches; a contractor may have already been in their living room with a quote that did not add up. They want a person — not a calculator — to look at their house and tell them what's worth doing.

Secondary visitors include curious daytime researchers comparing options, energy-savvy DIY homeowners cross-checking their own analysis, and spouses vetting after a contractor visit. The page must read as warm and credible to all four.

Job to be done: convert "I think I'm being ripped off by my utility, my contractor, or both" into "I just talked to someone who knows homes. He gave me a straight answer. I trust this."

## Product Purpose

ProFusion gives every home an Efficiency Score (a single underlying 400-to-800 number, exposed publicly only as a letter grade and an arc gauge), surfaces every rebate the home qualifies for at the federal, state, and utility level, and matches the homeowner with vetted installers. Currently active in 11 states (MA, RI, CT, ME, NH, VT, NY, NJ, MD, MI, FL), expanding nationwide.

Success: a homeowner on a phone in the evening drops their address, reads a friendly explanation of their grade and ranked next-best-upgrade with rebates already calculated, and either acts (we hand them a vetted installer) or saves the report and decides later. We win when the page feels like advice from someone they trust.

The numeric range (400 to 800) is intellectual property. Never expose it on the public site; always present the Score as a letter grade plus arc position.

## Brand Personality

**Warm. Plain-spoken. Patient. Quietly confident.**

The voice is that of a wise neighbor who's spent his career fixing homes and now spends afternoons explaining things to people who didn't grow up in the trades. He's calm. He's been doing this long enough that nothing is news. He uses the word "look" the way good explainers do, when he's about to clarify something. He doesn't say "leverage" or "utilize" or "synergy" or anything else a consultant would say.

Tagline: "Built for the house. Felt in the home."

Tone rules:

- **Plain English over jargon.** Every technical term is translated in the same sentence. *"Heat pump (it's a magic box that pulls warm air out of cold air)."* No glossary, no footnote, no "see definitions."
- **Conversational headlines.** Questions, declarations, observations. *"Before you say yes to a heat pump, talk to someone who's seen 1,200 of them."* Not *"Discover the Power of Heat Pump Optimization."*
- **Numbers when they earn their place.** A real dollar figure or a year is sharper than an adjective. But the numbers serve the explanation, not the other way around. We don't decorate copy with mono-rendered specs.
- **Italic serif (Instrument Serif) sparingly.** Two or three phrases per page where slowing the reader down does work. It is punctuation, not decoration.
- **Never urgency, scarcity, or social-proof manipulation.** No "JOIN 50,000+ HOMEOWNERS!" banners, no countdown clocks, no testimonial slideshows. The handyman doesn't need to convince you of anything; he just tells you what he sees.
- **Self-aware about expertise.** He doesn't perform competence — he just has it. Lines like *"I've seen this in about 80 of every 100 homes I look at"* land harder than *"Industry-leading expertise."*

What he sounds like: Norm Abram from This Old House, but with better web design. The Wirecutter writer who has tested 47 vacuums and tells you which one to buy. Your favorite Allrecipes commenter who says "I doubled the garlic and added a teaspoon of soy and it was perfect." A guru, not a salesman.

What he doesn't sound like: a consultant. A SaaS landing page. A solar-installer call script. An AI assistant.

## Anti-references

This page must not look or sound like:

1. **Typical solar-installer marketing.** Gradient mesh blobs, "FREE QUOTE!" buttons, before/after sliders, stock smiling-family photos, oversized testimonial slideshows. The category is saturated with bad faith.
2. **Crypto and AI-startup neon-on-black.** Terminal vibes, glitch effects, cyberpunk gradients, glowy borders, 3D blob shaders. ProFusion is not in the hype business.
3. **SaaS bento-grid with pastel mesh.** Asymmetric tile grid, soft purple-to-pink mesh background, oversized rounded-square icons above every heading. The 2024-to-2026 SaaS reflex; signals "AI made this."
4. **Government efficiency portals.** Information-dense but visually flat. Civic trust signaling executed like a 2008 PDF turned into a website.
5. **Engineering spec sheets, presented coldly.** Dense mono columns, "Input / Output / Cost" tables as the centerpiece, "Step 01 / Spec" framings. The math is real and important, but the surface should never feel like a NORAD console. We are engineers; we just don't dress like them on the front porch.
6. **Forced down-home folksiness.** Hand-drawn rope logos, "y'all"-coded copy, distressed wood textures, plaid backgrounds, kitchen-table cliché photography from a HomeAdvisor ad. The handyman is wise, not corny. The line between sophisticated warmth and Etsy-cabin is thin, and we stay well on the right side of it.

## Aesthetic family

We share visual DNA with: **Wirecutter** long-reads, **Real Simple** (modernized), **Patagonia** ambassador stories, **Allbirds** editorial, **NYT Cooking**, **Apartment Therapy** at its calmest, **This Old House** if it had been reskinned by Pentagram.

Photography direction: documentary editorial. Late-afternoon golden hour. Real homes, real seasons, real people doing real work. Warm light *inside* the photograph (the homes, the wood grain, the kitchens) carries the warmth the brand needs — the canvas around the photograph stays white. Slight 35mm film grain. Shallow depth of field. Never crossed-arms hero pose, never stock smiling family, never modern-glass-house surveillance imagery.

Typography direction: Geist as the modern workhorse stays. Instrument Serif italic stays for two-or-three earned moments per page. For longer-form passages where the handyman is "talking to you," we lean on **Source Serif 4** (Adobe, free on Google Fonts) so the long passages feel like a guidebook chapter rather than a marketing block. Mono is reserved for true data points only — never for spec-sheet decoration.

## Design Principles

1. **The math is the moat, but plain English is the currency.** Every number we show is real and computable. But the surface presentation is conversational, not clinical. If a treatment makes the math feel like a NORAD console, soften it.
2. **Warmth from voice, not from background tint.** The canvas stays white — clean, generous, editorial. Warmth comes from the things that live on the canvas: photography (golden-hour, real homes), a humanist serif (Source Serif 4) for long passages, and plain-spoken copy. The page should read warm even though its background is white.
3. **One moment of motion per page.** The Transformation tile is the only choreographed animation. Everything else holds still. Restraint is the brand stamp.
4. **Earn every accent.** Italic serif, color emphasis, and any other typographic accent appear in three places or fewer per page. A treatment used everywhere has zero gravity.
5. **Phone first, evening light.** The primary surface is a phone screen at 22:00 in dim ambient light. Text contrast, tap targets, and visual rhythm are tuned at that scale, in that ambient. Desktop is the upgrade, not the baseline.
6. **Anti-pitch.** No urgency, no scarcity, no testimonial spotlights, no social-proof manipulation. The handyman doesn't have to convince you of anything; he just tells you what he sees, and you decide.
7. **Start with Why.** Page narratives open with belief or observation, not with the product. The product enters as the natural answer to a question we've already raised.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **Target: WCAG 2.1 AA, full mobile-first.** Body text contrast 4.5:1 minimum; large text and UI elements 3:1 minimum. Tested from 320px viewport up.
- **Reduced motion** respected: `prefers-reduced-motion` bypasses choreographed scroll moments and paints the final state immediately.
- **Keyboard navigation** fully supported. Anything scroll-driven also reaches its final state via keyboard scroll; nothing requires hover or pointer.
- **Screen readers**: SVG illustrations carry meaningful aria-labels; counters use `aria-live="polite"` so the bill and grade announce once at scrub end (not on every tick).
- **Color blindness**: any state distinguishable by color must also be distinguished by shape, weight, position, or label. The Score gauge uses both a letter and a position on the arc.
- **Reading load**: copy targets roughly **Grade 6** reading level at the body (down from Grade 8). Technical terms are translated inline in the same sentence, never relegated to a glossary.
