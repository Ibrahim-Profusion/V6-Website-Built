04 · Install Timeline

From first call to permission to operate.

A typical New Jersey residential solar install runs 9–13 weeks from site survey to grid interconnection. Permitting and inspection sit on most of that runway.

01.
Site survey & system design
Contractor evaluates roof orientation, shade, structural load, and produces a system design with an annual production estimate.
Week 1
02.
Permitting & utility interconnection app
Building permits filed with the town; interconnection application filed with the local utility. Review typically runs 6–8 weeks.
Weeks 2-5
03.
Equipment procurement
Panels, inverter, racking, and balance-of-system components ordered after permit approval. Lead times depend on configuration.
Weeks 5-8
04.
Installation
Most residential installs complete in 1–3 days on-site. Building permit inspection follows a few days later.
Weeks 8-10
05.
Permission to operate (PTO)
Final utility inspection and net-metering activation. PTO is when the system legally begins exporting and earning credits.

06 · Questions Homeowners Ask

The honest FAQ.

Actual questions that come up in the first installer conversation.

Is the rate going down?

Yes, a 10% decrease was scheduled for March 13, 2026. Once enrolled, your rate locks for 15 years.

How is the SuSI rate set?

The rate is locked at the moment your system is registered with the BPU, and that rate holds for the full 15-year term. New registrations get the current rate; existing registrations keep whatever rate they locked in.

NJ · NJ Clean Energy Heat Pumps
Solar's biggest job is offsetting load. A heat pump roughly doubles your electric load (and zeros out fossil heat). Sized together, the array carries both.

NJ · NJ Comfort Partners
Insulation cuts the load before sizing the array. A tighter house needs fewer panels, which makes the whole project cheaper.
NJ · NJ Windows & Doors
ENERGY STAR windows trim cooling load through the summer (when your array produces most). Less load means more credit banking.
NJ · Garden State Energy Storage
Pair the array with a battery. Solar charges the battery midday, the battery powers the house at night, and the system islands automatically during outages.
