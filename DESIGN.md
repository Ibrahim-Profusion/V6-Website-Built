# Design System: ProFusion Home Solutions

## 1. Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Trusted Handyman, Engineering-Backed"**

The system reads like an afternoon with the wise old guy in the neighborhood that everyone calls before they say yes to a heat pump or a roof. He's seen 1,200 homes. He's done the math. He explains things plainly enough that a five-year-old gets it. Under the surface we are engineers — real underlying scoring model, real utility tariffs, real audited installer gates. On the surface, we are the trusted neighbor. The whole brand lives in that tension: **engineers under the hood, handyman at the door.**

Every visual choice serves that perception. Numbers stay real and computable, but the *presentation* is conversational, not clinical. **The canvas stays white** — clean, generous, Apple-editorial in its restraint. Warmth comes from the things that live *on* the canvas: photography (documentary editorial, golden-hour, real homes), typography (a humanist serif, Source Serif 4, for long passages where the handyman is talking to you), and copy (plain English, conversational headlines, no consultant-speak). The page should feel like a well-set-out guidebook, not a server room and not a kitchen-table cliché.

What this system explicitly rejects, in order: the bad-faith glossiness of typical solar-installer marketing; the hype theater of crypto and AI-startup neon-on-black; the SaaS-bento-grid reflex with its pastel mesh and identical icon tiles; the visually-flat civic-trust signaling of government efficiency portals; the **clinical engineering-spec-sheet aesthetic** (mono columns, "Input/Output/Cost" tables, "Step 01 · Spec" framings — the math is real but should never feel like a NORAD console); and **forced down-home folksiness** (hand-drawn rope logos, distressed wood textures, kitchen-table cliché HomeAdvisor photography). The handyman is wise, not corny.

We share a lane with **Wirecutter** long-reads, **Real Simple** (modernized), **Patagonia** ambassador stories, **Allbirds** editorial, **NYT Cooking**, **Apartment Therapy** at its calmest, and **This Old House** if it had been reskinned by Pentagram. Photography is documentary editorial: late-afternoon golden hour, real homes, real seasons, real people, slight 35mm grain. Never the smiling-family stock cliché.

**Key Characteristics:**

- One moment of motion per page (the Transformation tile). Everything else holds still.
- Restrained palette on a white canvas: tinted neutrals, gray-50 as the sparing tonal break, two committed accents (Public Trust Blue, Savings Green), one warm peach reserved for hero and Transformation glow.
- Type as rhythm. Geist for display and short-form body. **Source Serif 4** for long-form passages where the handyman is talking to you. Geist Mono is reserved for genuine data points only — never for spec-sheet decoration. Instrument Serif italic for the two or three phrases per page worth slowing down to read.
- Phone-first composition. The primary surface is a phone screen at 22:00 in dim ambient light. Desktop is the upgrade.
- No urgency, no scarcity, no testimonial spotlights. The page persuades by being correct *and* warm, not by being loud.

## 2. Colors

A palette of two committed accents on a tinted-neutral canvas, with warm peach reserved as a single emotional grace note. Total color budget: small. Restraint is the brand stamp.

### Primary

- **Public Trust Blue** (`#1A4F90`): the brand's spine. Carries institutional trust on hero gradient endpoints, primary link color, italic emphasis when the meaning is identity-related (state names, the Score itself, "your home"). Used at full strength only on type and small UI accents; never as a flat background fill on a section.
- **Public Trust Blue Deep** (`#0A2E5C`): for hero gradient anchor, dark-section backgrounds (final CTA glow, footer stripe).
- **Public Trust Blue Mid** (`#2E6DB8`): map-cell active gradient endpoint; never on text under Body weight.
- **Public Trust Blue Tint** (`#EAF3FC`): chip and eyebrow background variant, info callout backgrounds.

### Secondary

- **Savings Green** (`#3AB44A`): the system's "good news" register. Carries savings totals, rebate-stack callouts, the Score after the upgrade, the eyebrow dot. Earned, not decorative.
- **Savings Green Deep** (`#2E9A3D`): green eyebrow text, "good" badge text, link-state success indicators.
- **Savings Green Tint** (`#EEFAF0`): green-good badge background, callout fill for positive outcomes.

### Tertiary

- **Warm Peach Hero** (`#FFE7CC`) and **Warm Peach Deeper** (`#FFD4A8`): the only colors permitted on full-bleed surface fills outside the standard white/gray-50 alternation. Used in two places: the hero illustration backdrop and the Transformation final-state glow. Their scarcity is what makes them mean "the warm part of the day." White is the canvas; peach is a singular emotional grace note.

### Alert

- **Alert Red** (`#B91C1C`): the "today" register. Reserved for the pre-upgrade grade letter, the rising annual-bill counter, and any element that represents the cost of doing nothing. Never used decoratively.
- **Alert Red Tint** (`#FEE8E8`): red badge background only.

### Neutral

- **Ink** (`#0A1020`): primary body and headline text. Almost-black with a faint blue undertone. Never use `#000`.
- **Neutral 800** (`#1E2434`): nav link text, body intensifications.
- **Neutral 600** (`#4A5266`): subhead and secondary body color.
- **Neutral 400** (`#8891A5`): meta text, captions, dim labels.
- **Neutral 200** (`#D6DCE7`): subtle dividers, hairline borders on hover-state.
- **Neutral 100** (`#E8ECF3`): default hairline border on cards and dividers.
- **Neutral 50** (`#F5F7FB`): grey-band section background, inner card surface.
- **Off-White** (`#FAFBFD`): rarely used; reserved for absolute neutral surfaces like footer.
- **White** (`#FFFFFF`): canvas default.

### Named Rules

**The Earned Accent Rule.** Public Trust Blue, Savings Green, and the blue-to-green gradient italic each appear in **three places or fewer per page**. A treatment used everywhere has zero gravity. The gradient italic earns its three slots: hero h1, Transformation result, final CTA. Everywhere else, italic serif drops to flat Public Trust Blue.

**The One Tonal Break Rule.** White is the canvas. Gray-50 is the only tonal break, and it appears once or twice per page, never eight times. Chained alternation reads as a Wordpress theme. Hero through Transformation through Three Slices stays on white as one editorial movement; the dense info zone (How, Map) gets a single grey band; final CTA returns to white. The warmth of the page comes from photography, typography, and voice — not from tinted backgrounds.

**The Warm Peach Reservation.** Warm Peach Hero and Warm Peach Deeper appear only on the hero illustration backdrop and the Transformation final-state radial glow. Nowhere else, ever.

## 3. Typography

**Display Font:** Geist (Google Fonts) with `-apple-system, sans-serif` fallback.
**Body Font:** Geist (carries short-form body — leads, captions, UI copy).
**Body Serif Font:** **Source Serif 4** (Google Fonts) with `Georgia, serif` fallback. Reserved for long-form passages where the handyman is "talking to you" — section ledes that span 60–80 words, deliverable explanations, why-it-matters sections. Adds warmth and a guidebook cadence without going nostalgic; NOT used for short labels or UI text.
**Label / Mono Font:** Geist Mono with `ui-monospace, monospace` fallback.
**Italic Emphasis:** Instrument Serif (Google Fonts) with `Georgia, serif` fallback. Italic style only.

**Character:** Geist is the modern workhorse — calm, neutral, expressive at scale. **Source Serif 4** is the wise voice — humanist serif by Frank Grießhammer at Adobe, designed for editorial reading, warm without nostalgic, pairs cleanly with Geist (similar x-height, modernist proportions). Instrument Serif italic stays as the grace note for two-or-three earned phrases per page. Geist Mono is *only* for genuine data — bill counters, years, percentages — never as spec-sheet decoration.

### Hierarchy

- **Display** (Geist 500, `clamp(56px, 9vw, 124px)`, line-height 0.94, letter-spacing -0.05em): hero h1 only.
- **Headline** (Geist 500, `clamp(40px, 6.4vw, 80px)`, line-height 1.0, letter-spacing -0.04em): section h2.
- **Title** (Geist 500, 22px, line-height 1.2, letter-spacing -0.02em): card headings, step titles.
- **Body** (Geist 400, `clamp(18px, 1.5vw, 22px)`, line-height 1.55, max-width ~640px): subheads, short-form leads, UI copy.
- **Body Serif** (Source Serif 4 400, `clamp(17px, 1.4vw, 20px)`, line-height 1.6, max-width ~620px): long-form passages where the handyman explains something. Used in: hero lede, the "why" passage, the deliverable explanation, the closing CTA passage. Two or three placements per page max.
- **Body Small** (Geist 400, 15px, line-height 1.6): captions, footer copy, narrator italics.
- **Emphasis Italic** (Instrument Serif italic, weight 400): single phrases inside Display, Headline, Title, Body, or Body Serif. Punctuation, not decoration.
- **Label** (Geist Mono 600, 12px, letter-spacing 0.14em, uppercase): eyebrows, step markers, section status meta. Numbers + code-like data only.
- **Numeric** (Geist Mono 600, 13px, letter-spacing 0.04em): bill counters, percentages, year markers. Always `font-feature-settings: "tnum"`.

### Named Rules

**The Mono-for-Numbers Rule.** Geist Mono is reserved for numeric data and code-like labels. **It is not a decoration font.** Do not use it on body copy, section titles, or as a "spec sheet" framing device (`Step 01 · Spec`-style headers, `Time / Input / Output / Cost` tables, etc.). If you find yourself reaching for mono to make something feel "engineered," reach for Source Serif 4 instead — that's the engineer-handyman voice. Mono only when the substance is genuinely a number or a code-like value.

**The Earned Italic Rule.** Instrument Serif italic appears at most three times in a single section, and at most a dozen times on the entire page. It is a hand on the shoulder, not a recurring decoration.

**The Long-Form Serif Rule.** Source Serif 4 is reserved for passages that read like a guidebook chapter — paragraphs of 40+ words where the voice is "the handyman explaining something." It is not for short labels or UI affordances. Two or three placements per page; if a fourth is creeping in, the page is leaning too hard on the device. Geist body remains the default for everything shorter and structural.

**The Body Line Length Rule.** Body and lead paragraph max-width: 640px on Geist, ~620px on Source Serif 4. On wide layouts, the column never expands to fill.

## 4. Elevation

Flat by default. Lift on state. Surfaces rest at zero elevation; shadows are reserved as a response to interaction (hover, focus) and to anchor the two signature visual moments (the hero cartoon and the Transformation card). The system does not "float" by default. It sits on the page like type on paper.

### Shadow Vocabulary

- **shadow-sm** (`0 1px 2px rgba(10, 16, 32, 0.04), 0 1px 3px rgba(10, 16, 32, 0.05)`): hairline lift on hover. Used on card hover, button hover. Almost imperceptible at rest, deliberate as a response.
- **shadow-md** (`0 6px 20px -6px rgba(10, 16, 32, 0.10), 0 2px 6px rgba(10, 16, 32, 0.06)`): card hover end-state, primary CTA elevated focus.
- **shadow-lg** (`0 28px 56px -16px rgba(10, 16, 32, 0.16), 0 10px 20px -6px rgba(10, 16, 32, 0.08)`): structural anchor only. Reserved for the hero cartoon container and the Transformation report card. Two moments per page.
- **shadow-xl** (`0 48px 96px -24px rgba(10, 16, 32, 0.22), 0 18px 36px -10px rgba(10, 16, 32, 0.12)`): defined in tokens, currently unused. Hold in reserve for future signature moments.

### Named Rules

**The Flat-By-Default Rule.** Cards, callouts, eyebrows, badges, nav, the stat strip, the service map, the final CTA: all flat at rest. A 1px hairline border (`var(--neutral-100)`) does the visual separation work that a shadow would do in a weaker system.

**The Two-Anchor Rule.** Only two elements on the entire page carry resting elevation: the hero cartoon (shadow-lg) and the Transformation report card (shadow-lg). They are the page's gravitational centers. Elevating a third element steals weight from the two that earn it.

## 5. Components

### Buttons

- **Shape:** pill, `rounded.pill` (100px radius). The CTA is round-ended on purpose. It is the only element on the page with full-pill radius outside the nav and eyebrows.
- **Primary:** ink background (`#0A1020`), white text, 18px / 32px padding, weight 500, font-size 16px, letter-spacing -0.01em. Carries `box-shadow: 0 4px 16px -4px rgba(10, 16, 32, 0.25)` at rest as a single intentional exception to The Flat-By-Default Rule (the CTA is the page's call to action; it earns weight).
- **Hover / Focus:** background transitions to `linear-gradient(135deg, var(--public-trust-blue), var(--savings-green-deep))`, transform: translateY(-2px), shadow grows to `0 8px 24px -4px rgba(26, 79, 144, 0.35)`. The right-arrow chevron inside the button translates `+4px` on hover.
- **Text Button (link-style):** the secondary affordance. Geist 500, 15px, color Public Trust Blue. On hover, the gap between text and trailing chevron grows from 6px to 10px. No background, no border, no shadow.

### Eyebrows

- **Style:** mono uppercase label preceded by a 6px Savings Green dot. Geist Mono 600, 12px, letter-spacing 0.14em, color Savings Green Deep. The dot uses `border-radius: 50%`, `background: var(--savings-green)`. No pill background. No accent background fill behind the text.
- **Placement:** one per section, sitting 24px above the section h2.
- **Variant for Stages:** numeric only ("01" / "02" / "STEP 01"). Same type treatment, no dot. Used inside the Transformation tile.

### Cards

- **Corner Style:** `rounded.md` (20px) for slices, promises, How steps. `rounded.lg` (28px) for hero art container and Transformation report card.
- **Background:** white. Inside grey-band sections, cards remain white (the contrast is the design).
- **Shadow Strategy:** flat at rest. shadow-sm on hover. The two structural exceptions (hero cartoon, Transformation card) carry shadow-lg at rest.
- **Border:** 1px solid Neutral 100. Hairline. Increases to Neutral 200 on hover-state where lift is signaled.
- **Internal Padding:** 32px on standard cards, 44px on feature cards (Promises). The padding rhythm is part of the hierarchy: more padding = higher surface importance.

### Navigation

- **Style:** slim pill, fixed at top, frosted-glass blur (`backdrop-filter: blur(24px) saturate(1.8)`), 78% white background opacity. Pill radius 100px. Sits 16px from the viewport top, max-width 1280px, centered.
- **Typography:** Geist 500, 14px, letter-spacing default, color Neutral 800.
- **Default state:** hairline Neutral 100 border, shadow-sm.
- **Scrolled state:** border deepens to Neutral 200, shadow grows to shadow-md. No layout shift, only state-shift.
- **Mobile (<= 760px):** middle nav links collapse; the CTA pill remains.
- **Active link:** rounded-pill ghost background (`var(--neutral-100)` at 0.6 opacity).

### Score-Letter Badge (signature component)

The Score letter is the page's most concentrated brand signal. It is a typographic badge, not a generic chip.

- **Pre-upgrade (Today's grade):** Instrument Serif italic 600, 80px, letter-spacing -0.04em, color Alert Red, on Alert Red Tint background, padding 6px 24px, `rounded.sm`.
- **Post-upgrade (After):** same treatment, color Savings Green Deep, on Savings Green Tint background.
- **Transition:** when the Transformation tile lands at progress 0.92, both background and color cross-fade over 600ms cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.9, 0.3, 1). The transition is the moment of visual payoff.

### Stat Strip

- **Style:** single mono line, hairline borders top and bottom (`1px solid var(--neutral-100)`).
- **Type:** Geist Mono 500, 13px, letter-spacing 0.06em, color Neutral 600. `<strong>` elements ink-colored, weight 600.
- **Padding:** 56px vertical (intentionally tight, signals "caption" rather than "section").

## 6. Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- **Do** earn every accent. Gradient italic, Public Trust Blue emphasis, Savings Green emphasis: each appears three times per page or fewer. Restraint is the gravity.
- **Do** reserve Geist Mono for numbers and code-like labels. Never use it on body copy, section titles, or "spec sheet" framings.
- **Do** reach for **Source Serif 4** when the voice is the handyman explaining something — long-form passages, why-it-matters paragraphs, the deliverable description. Two or three placements per page.
- **Do** treat **white** as the canvas and **gray-50** as the only tonal break, used once or twice per page. Warmth comes from photography, typography, and copy — not from tinted backgrounds.
- **Do** keep cards flat at rest. Shadow appears only on hover, focus, or as a state-change response. Two structural exceptions (hero illustration, Transformation card) anchor the page.
- **Do** size phone-first. Test every layout at 320px and at 22:00 dim-light contrast before validating on desktop.
- **Do** anchor the Transformation tile as the single moment of motion on the page. Every other section is intentionally still.
- **Do** translate every technical term inline in the same sentence. *"Heat pump (it's a magic box that pulls warm air out of cold air)."* The handyman never sends you to a glossary.
- **Do** write conversational headlines. Questions, declarations, observations the handyman would say out loud. *"Before you say yes to a heat pump, talk to someone who's seen 1,200 of them."* Not *"Discover Heat Pump Optimization Solutions."*
- **Do** target **Grade 6** reading level on body copy. The wise neighbor explains things plainly enough that a five-year-old gets the gist.
- **Do** use commas, colons, semicolons, periods, and parentheses for prose punctuation. Em dashes are out of voice for this system.
- **Do** use tabular figures (`font-feature-settings: "tnum"`) on every counter, bill amount, and score number. Numbers must align as data, not as type.
- **Do** specify photography as documentary editorial — late-afternoon golden hour, real homes, real seasons, slight 35mm grain, shallow DOF. Save placeholders to `images/*-handyman-placeholder.jpg` so real photography swaps in cleanly later.
- **Do** treat alt text as part of the design. SVG illustrations and photographs both carry meaningful aria-labels. Counters use `aria-live="polite"`.

### Don't:

- **Don't** mesh-gradient, blob, or pastel-glow anything. PRODUCT.md rejects "typical solar-installer marketing" by name; mesh gradients are the visual surface of that rejection.
- **Don't** stack neon-on-black, glitch effects, terminal aesthetics, or 3D blob shaders. PRODUCT.md rejects "crypto and AI-startup neon-on-black"; this is the visual line.
- **Don't** build a bento grid. Asymmetric tile grids with rounded-square icon tiles signal "AI made this."
- **Don't** flatten the page into a civic-trust portal. Right values, wrong execution. Editorial confidence and warmth are the antidote.
- **Don't render evidence as a spec sheet.** No "Step 01 · Spec" headers. No "Time / Input / Output / Cost" tables as the centerpiece of a section. No mono columns of field-value pairs treated as decorative architecture. The math is real and important, but the surface should never feel like a NORAD console. If you're tempted to render a piece of evidence as a spec block, render it as the handyman explaining it instead.
- **Don't** lean on hand-drawn rope, distressed wood textures, plaid backgrounds, or any other forced down-home folksiness. The handyman is wise, not corny. The line between sophisticated warmth and Etsy-cabin is thin; stay well on the right side.
- **Don't** use `#000` or `#fff` directly outside the canvas. `#0A1020` (Ink) is the black; `#FFFFFF` is the white canvas; tint every neutral toward the brand.
- **Don't** alternate white and gray-50 sections more than twice per page. One grey moment for density, white for everything else. Eight alternations is a Wordpress theme.
- **Don't** apply gradient italic outside the three earned slots (hero h1, Transformation result, final CTA). Anywhere else, italic serif is flat Public Trust Blue.
- **Don't** use side-stripe borders (`border-left: 4px solid` accents). They are decorative and never intentional. Use full hairline borders, leading numerics, or icon prefixes instead.
- **Don't** put gradient backgrounds behind text and use `background-clip: text`. Outside the three earned slots, gradient text is decorative and out of voice.
- **Don't** wrap the How-It-Works steps in cards. The handyman doesn't hand you a pamphlet; he tells you in plain words.
- **Don't** use modal dialogs as a first thought. Most reveals can happen inline.
- **Don't** use bounce or elastic easing curves. Use ease-out exponential (cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.9, 0.3, 1) or ease-out-quart). Bounce reads as 2014 app.
- **Don't** animate CSS layout properties (width, height, padding, margin). Animate transforms, opacity, and stroke-dashoffset only.
- **Don't** put em dashes in copy or component prose. Use commas, colons, semicolons, periods, parentheses.
- **Don't** ship cliché stock photography (smiling crossed-arms contractor, perfect family in front of perfect house, modern-glass-house surveillance imagery). If real photography isn't ready, use clearly-marked illustrated placeholders instead — they age better than bad stock.
- **Don't** expose the Score's underlying numeric range (400 to 800). Always present the Score as a letter grade plus arc position. The range is intellectual property.
